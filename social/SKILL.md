# Social Content

Help the user create posts for TikTok, Instagram and LinkedIn that sound like a real person talking. Many users are creators who are new to marketing and new to AI. Use plain words, skip jargon, and explain a term the first time you use one.

Storytelling comes first. A specific story, number or moment from the user's own life beats any tactic below. The tactics help a good story travel. They never replace one.

## Before You Write

Ask only what you need, in one short message. Skip anything the user already told you.

1. **Platform**: TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn, or all three?
2. **Goal**: grow followers, get comments, get people to a link, or teach something?
3. **Audience**: who should this reach?
4. **The real details**: what actually happened, what they used, what it cost, how long it took.
5. **Voice**: casual, funny, calm, or more professional? Any words they always or never use?

Never invent facts about the user. No made-up numbers, results, people or stories. If a detail is missing, leave a placeholder like `[how much you spent]` and ask for it.

## Default Output

When the user gives you an idea and nothing else, return:

1. **3 hook options** in different styles (see Hook Patterns).
2. **The main piece**: a video script, carousel or post, depending on the platform.
3. **A caption for each platform** they're posting to.
4. **Hashtags**: 3 to 5, specific to the topic.

Keep it short. Offer the other platforms as a follow-up instead of writing everything at once.

## Platform Quick Reference

| Platform | Best for | How often | Main formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Reach, new audiences, tutorials, storytimes | 1 a day to start; more once posts get steady views | Short video, photo carousels |
| Instagram | Community, visual brands, saves and shares | 1 to 2 posts a day, plus Stories | Reels, carousels, Stories |
| LinkedIn | Career, work stories, professional credibility | 3 to 5 a week | Text posts, document carousels |

**TikTok.** Raw and unpolished beats produced. Hook in the first 1 to 2 seconds. Vertical 9:16 only. Reply to comments with videos. Trending sounds help lifestyle content; talking-head tutorials use original audio.

**Instagram.** Reels get the most reach. Saves and shares count more than likes. The first frame of a Reel has to hook on its own. Carousels work well for step-by-steps people want to keep. Stories keep you in front of people who already follow you.

**LinkedIn.** The first two lines show before "see more", so the hook has to land there. Personal work stories with a clear lesson do best. Put links in the first comment, not the post. Write in paragraphs, not one sentence per line. Around 1,200 to 1,500 characters is a good length.

### Limits

These were accurate when the original skill was written. Check the platform if a number matters.

| | Instagram | TikTok | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max caption | 2,200 chars | 4,000 chars | 3,000 chars |
| Shows before "more" | ~125 chars | ~150 chars | ~210 chars |
| Hashtags | 5 max | 5 max | 3 to 5 suggested, at the end |

Front-load the point before the cut-off. Hashtags count toward the character limit.

## Content Pillars

Help the user pick 3 to 5 topics they can post about again and again. Example for a creator teaching AI to beginners:

| Pillar | Share | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| How-tos | 35% | Step-by-steps, exact prompts, tool walkthroughs |
| Real life | 25% | What they used AI for this week, wins and fails |
| Behind the scenes | 20% | What they're building or learning |
| Opinions | 15% | Takes on tools, trends, myths |
| Offers | 5% | Their product, newsletter or link |

For each pillar ask: what do people keep asking you, what have you posted that did well, and what could you talk about every week?

## Hook Patterns

The first line or first second decides whether anyone stays. A hook is a starting shape. Fill it with a real detail from the user.

### Creator voice hooks

Casual, first person, like talking to a friend on camera. Default to these for TikTok and Reels.

- "I spent $[X] last month and told myself, mmm, I need to budget a little better."
- "You asked for the breakdown, so here it is."
- "Okay so I finally tried [thing] and I have thoughts."
- "My [sister/friend/mom] asked how I [did thing], so now everyone gets to see."
- "If you're not techy at all, this one's for you."
- "I used to spend [time] on [task] every week. Now it takes me [shorter time]."
- "Come with me while I [build/plan/fix] [thing] using AI."
- "I'm going to show you exactly what I typed."
- "Three things I wish someone told me before I started [thing]."
- "Storytime. The day [unexpected thing] happened."
- "This is your sign to stop doing [task] by hand."
- "Real quick, here's how I [outcome] without [annoying part]."
- "I tested this for [time] so you don't have to."

### Classic hooks

Work on every platform, and suit LinkedIn best.

- **Curiosity**: "I was wrong about [common belief]." / "[Result] in [surprisingly short time]."
- **Story**: "Last week, [unexpected thing] happened." / "[Time] ago, I [old state]. Today, [new state]."
- **Value**: "How to [outcome] without [common pain]." / "[Number] [things] that [outcome]."
- **Contrarian**: "Unpopular opinion: [bold statement]." / "I stopped [common practice] and [result]."
- **Warning**: "Stop [common mistake]. Here's what to do instead."
- **Social proof**: "[Number] people asked me about [topic]. Here's my answer."

## Write Like a Person

People scroll past posts that read as AI-written. Never use these:

- **Contrast reveals**: "It's not X, it's Y." "Not because X. Because Y." Just say Y and why.
- **Negation lists**: "No X, no Y, no Z." Say what does happen.
- **Self-answered questions and colon reveals**: "The result? 3x faster." "The best part: it learns."
- **Stock phrases**: "game-changer", "unlock the power of", "take it to the next level", "here's the thing", "let that sink in", "say goodbye to".
- **Em dashes**. Use a period or a line break.
- **Broetry**: one short sentence per line for the whole post. Write paragraphs and break where a person would pause.
- **Engagement bait**: "Agree?" "Thoughts?" "Read that again." End on the point, or a real question you want answered.
- **Fake vulnerability**: "I'll be honest, I was terrified..." with no real story behind it.
- **Emoji bullets**: an emoji starting every line.

Use contractions. Keep lists of three to one per post. Read the script out loud in your head. If it doesn't sound like talking, rewrite it.

## Short-Form Video (TikTok and Reels)

Default to **60 to 75 seconds**, about 150 to 190 spoken words. Go shorter only if the user asks or the idea is a single quick tip. The same video can go to TikTok and Reels.

### Hook the first second

Every video needs three hooks that land together in the first second:

1. **Visual**: something moving, a result on screen, a face mid-sentence.
2. **Spoken**: the hook line.
3. **On-screen text**: the hook in 3 to 6 words, for people watching on mute.

### Structures (60 to 75 seconds)

**Tutorial**
```
[0-3s]   Hook: show the finished result
[3-10s]  Why it matters or who it's for
[10-60s] Steps, one clear beat each, show the screen
[60-75s] Result again + one CTA
```

**Storytime**
```
[0-3s]   Hook: tease the outcome
[3-15s]  Setup: where you were, what was wrong
[15-50s] What happened, with real details
[50-65s] How it turned out
[65-75s] The lesson + one CTA
```

**List**
```
[0-3s]   Hook: "[N] things that [outcome]"
[3-65s]  One item every 10 to 15 seconds
[65-75s] Which one to try first + CTA
```

**Problem and fix**
```
[0-3s]   Hook: name the problem
[3-15s]  Why it's annoying or costly
[15-60s] The fix, shown step by step
[60-75s] Result + CTA
```

### Script template

```
Video: [working title]
Platform: TikTok / Reels
Length: [60-75] sec
Format: talking head / screen recording / voiceover over b-roll

HOOK (0-3s)
Visual: [what's on screen]
Say: [hook line]
Text: [on-screen text]

BODY
[timestamp] Say: [line]  |  Show: [screen or b-roll]
[timestamp] Say: [line]  |  Show: [...]

CTA (last 5-10s)
Say: [one ask: follow, comment a word, save, link in bio]
Text: [on-screen text]

NOTES
Sound: [original audio or a trending sound]
B-roll / screenshots needed: [list]
```

### Captions on screen

Most people watch on mute. On-screen captions should be:

- 2 lines max at a time
- 3 to 5 words per line
- Bold sans-serif with an outline, key words in a different color
- Timed to the speech

Free tool: CapCut auto-captions.

### Common mistakes

1. Slow start. Don't build up to the point.
2. No on-screen text.
3. Bad audio. Use a quiet room and a cheap mic if possible.
4. More than one CTA.
5. Ignoring comments in the first hour.

## Carousels (Instagram and LinkedIn)

Slide 1 is the thumbnail. It has to stop the scroll on its own. One idea per slide, same layout on every slide, one CTA on the last slide. Pick a shape before writing:

| The content is... | Shape | Slides |
|---|---|---|
| A list of tools, prompts or tips | **Exact count**: "7 prompts for [outcome]", one per slide, then CTA | 5 to 10 |
| A result with a system behind it | **Proof**: the result, the real problem, the steps, the receipt (a screenshot) | 6 to 10 |
| A workflow you can show | **Walkthrough**: finished result, the pain it fixes, step overview, one screenshot per step | 5 to 10 |
| A strong opinion | **Rant**: the claim, specific examples, "don't get me wrong" fairness slide, sign-off | 4 to 8 |

Deliver exactly the count the cover promises. Use 4:5 slides (1080 x 1350) on Instagram. On LinkedIn, upload as a PDF and write the post text as its own hook. Judge carousels by saves, not likes.

## Repurposing: One Idea, Three Platforms

Start from one idea, video, voice note or long post. Pull out the "content atoms", the moments that work on their own:

| Atom | Looks like | Best fit |
|---|---|---|
| Quick tip | One how-to, explained clearly | TikTok, Reels, LinkedIn post |
| Story | Setup, problem, what happened, result | TikTok, Reels, LinkedIn story post |
| Exact steps or prompts | Something people want to keep | Instagram or LinkedIn carousel |
| Hot take | An opinion people will argue with | LinkedIn post, TikTok |
| Behind the scenes | Messy, real, in progress | Instagram Stories, TikTok |

Workflow:

1. Pick the atoms (3 to 5 from one longer piece).
2. Make the video once and post it to TikTok and Reels.
3. Turn the steps into a carousel.
4. Turn the story or lesson into a LinkedIn post, in paragraphs, more context, less slang.
5. Write a fresh caption for each. Each post has to make sense on its own.
6. Spread them over the week instead of posting all at once.

### LinkedIn post shapes

**Story post**: hook, when and where, the problem, what you tried, the turning point, the result, the lesson, a real question.

**How-to post**: "How to [outcome] in [time]", 3 to 5 steps with one line of why for each, the result to expect, one CTA.

**List post**: "[N] things I learned about [topic] after [real experience]", each with a one-line explanation, a closing thought.

## Engagement

A short daily routine:

1. Reply to every comment on your recent posts.
2. Leave 5 to 10 real comments on creators in your space. Add something, don't just say "great post".
3. Reply to a good comment with a video (TikTok and Reels).

## What to Track

- **Saves and shares**: was it worth keeping or sending?
- **Watch time and completion**: did the hook and script hold people?
- **Comments**: did it start a conversation?
- **Follows and profile visits**: did people want more?

If a few videos in a row lose people in the first 3 seconds, change the hook. If views are high but follows are low, check the CTA and whether the topic fits the account. Test one change at a time: hook, length, format or posting time.

## When They're Stuck

- Answer a question someone asked in the comments or DMs.
- Show what they did with AI this week, start to finish.
- Redo an old post that did well with a new hook.
- Share a mistake and what they'd do differently.

## Scheduling

If the user has a scheduling tool connected, create posts as drafts first. Never publish or schedule anything without the user's clear go-ahead.
